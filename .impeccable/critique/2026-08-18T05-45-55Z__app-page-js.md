## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored for this product — but carried by conceit, not by fact.**

The hallmark conceit is genuinely welded to the company: *Diaphora* is the differentia, a hallmark is a struck differentia, and the clockmaker's signed-dial/punched-movement split maps exactly onto Delta 0 versus Delta 1. A rival could not run "an incubator that marks, not one that ranks" without stealing the Greek name it depends on.

The caveat is severe. Strip the plaque and **every word of the hero is metaphor.** The words *border*, *Canada*, *cross-border* and *United States* appear nowhere in the visible hero — only in the `<meta>` description. The hero argues taxonomy when PRODUCT principle 3 says place *is* the argument.

**Deterministic scan:** `app/page.js` is clean — zero findings. One project-wide warning, `layout-transition` at `components/Axis.module.css:62` (`transition: width`), outside the hero.

**Browser evidence:** no horizontal overflow at 1440 or 390, no page errors, single h1, no skipped heading levels. Three contrast failures, all outside the hero copy: axis ordinals at **2.99:1** (9.6px), axis labels and the mark-row caption at **4.45:1**. h1 line-height is **0.94** — leading below font size. Mobile axis clips DELTA 2 and WATERFALL off the right edge with no scroll affordance. The two 64px door CTAs are `<span>`, not headings — the largest type on the page is absent from the heading outline, and renders 1.67× larger than the h1 it semantically outranks.

## Overall Impression

The best copy on this page is very good, and it is in the wrong place. "No application, no cohort, no permission" is a real, generous, specific claim that answers what a founder actually wants to know — and it sits below the fold on mobile, behind fourteen unlabeled glyphs, after a four-sentence metaphor lesson. The hero teaches a conceit before it makes an offer, and closes on "One you are granted": passive voice, unnamed grantor, deference to a gate. DESIGN.md says the counterweight to cold struck metal "is copy voice, not decoration." The hero copy doubles the cold instead.

Biggest opportunity: move one fact — the border — into the first sentence, and let the metaphor follow it rather than precede it.

## What's Working

1. **"Take your mark" / "Be assayed" as paired verbs.** Two doors, two grammatical voices: active/self-directed against submitted-to. The syntax *is* the product difference. Almost no accelerator site makes grammar work this hard.
2. **The Delta 0 door refuses to inflate.** Three negations, each a real product fact, each a swipe at the batch model without naming YC. This is "credible before impressive" in practice.
3. **The plaque's assay line positions quietly.** Coordinates assert a specific physical place with surveyor's precision instead of a marketing adjective, and read across all four audiences without translation.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The border is missing from the hero.** The one thing a neighbouring incubator cannot truthfully copy appears in the first viewport only as latitude and longitude. The meta description says "at the Canada/USA border"; the visible page never does.
Fix — rewrite the lede to lead with the fact and let the conceit follow: "Diaphora Labs is a startup incubator on the Canada–US border at Niagara Falls. A clockmaker signs the dial in the open, and punches the movement where only an opened case will show it. We keep both. Delta 0 is the mark you take yourself — free, open to anyone on earth. Delta 1 is the mark you are granted, in person, at the line."
Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P1] "Be assayed" is undefined at the moment it is asked for.** The word carries the whole Delta 1 proposition, set in display type, glossed nowhere in the viewport. Do not remove it — define it on first use: "Assay is the old word for testing metal against a standard; Delta 1 does it to companies."
Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P1] Three of four audiences finish the first screen with nothing addressed to them.** Both doors are founder-shaped. "Partners" appears once, inside the mark-row readout, as a values statement rather than an invitation. Investors and ministers get nothing. Fix: a third, quieter ruled row beneath the doors — "Not a founder? Delta 1 is unfunded and looking for partners. Project: Waterfall is a live proposal to Niagara Parks." Bone, not current; subordinate but present. No fabrication required.
Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P2] "Marks, not ranks" is contradicted 700px later by "Selective · In person · TRL assessed".** A program that assesses TRL and grants marks selectively is ranking. Name what it actually refuses: "An incubator that marks. Not one that ranks you against a batch."
Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P2] Mobile first screen carries no offer and no action.** At 390×844 the plaque runs to 636px, the h1 lands at 338, and the doors start at 910 — entirely below the fold. The generous Delta 0 copy and both CTAs are unreachable on the first screen. Fix: reduce the plaque display size on narrow, or move the mark row below the doors so the doors compete for the fold.
Suggested command: /impeccable adapt

## Persona Red Flags

**Deputy minister, 20 seconds, phone, between meetings.** Reads a name, coordinates, "an incubator that marks not one that ranks", and a clockmaker. *Canada* is not a word on this page. Nothing about jurisdiction, regional impact, or institutional standing survives the skim. The one signal built for this reader — the plaque's civic-tablet form — does the whole job unassisted, and costs 580px of a phone screen to do it.

**Corporate/funding partner evaluating credibility.** Delta 1 is unfunded and this site is how that conversation starts, yet the hero makes no offer to them and names no route. The only door that could be theirs says "Be assayed" — positioning the reader as the one being tested. The hero's closing words, "One you are granted," compound it: an authority conferring status, not an organisation seeking a partner. Three real supporters exist and none is visible here.

**First-time founder in Lagos or Manila — Delta 0's actual target.** The best copy on the page is for them and they may never reach it: below the fold on mobile, behind fourteen unlabeled glyphs, gated by a metaphor chain in a second language. "The punch is free" is the only use of *free* and it is metaphorical, leaving the pricing question open exactly where it should close.

## Minor Observations

- `.thesis em` conveys emphasis on "marks" **by colour alone** (`font-style: normal`). Contrast is fine at 14.38:1, but colour-only emphasis fails WCAG 1.4.1, and chartreuse is specified in DESIGN.md to mark what is *live* — a word inside an h1 is not on that list. The detector did not catch this.
- The plaque presents `DELTA 0 · DELTA 1 · DELTA 2` as three equal programs on the most authoritative object on the page. Delta 2 does not exist.
- "Diaphora keeps both" — brand commitments specify *Diaphora Labs*. Use the full name at least once in the hero.
- The mark-row idle readout states a count, not a meaning, and never says these are *values*. It is also the hardest-to-read setting on the page (uppercase mono, 12.8px, 0.1em tracking) carrying the line that has to justify fourteen glyphs.
- `aria-live="polite"` on the mark readout fires on every hover across fourteen buttons; a screen-reader user sweeping the row hears fourteen names with no preceding explanation of what the row is.
- Delta 0's meta line ("Open · Global · No gate") repeats the body. It could carry a fact the body doesn't.
- No h1-level statement of what Diaphora Labs *does*. "Incubator" is the only functional noun, and it arrives inside a contrastive construction rather than a definition.

## Questions to Consider

1. If you deleted the clockmaker sentence entirely, what would a reader lose about the *offer*, as opposed to the pleasure of a good metaphor?
2. The hero never says Canada or border — but your meta description does. Which one did you write for the reader?
3. "Marks, not ranks", then TRL assessment and selection. Which of those would you defend to a founder you rejected?
4. Is the home page a manifesto or a switchboard? It is currently a manifesto on a site whose stated job is routing four audiences.
5. Name the warmest sentence in the first viewport. Is it warmer than "One you are granted"?
